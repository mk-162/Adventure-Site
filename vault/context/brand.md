# Adventure Wales — Brand & Strategy

Distilled from `STRATEGY.md` (Feb 2026, still current). Full detail there.

## What it is

A **trip-planning platform** for outdoor adventures in Wales — not a listings directory,
not a tourism board. Core insight: nobody books just one activity; they book a *trip*
(activity + stay + food + rainy-day backup + logistics). We connect the pieces.

## Audience

1. **Trip Planner** (primary) — couples/groups/families, "something outdoorsy in Wales",
   need the full picture and confidence to book.
2. **Activity Booker** — knows what they want, needs the right operator + trust signals.
3. **Operator** — Welsh adventure businesses wanting qualified leads. Free listing = found;
   paid = featured.

## USP

**Intelligent itineraries**: interactive plans with real operators/prices, matched
accommodation, food, attractions, transport, weather, and local tips. If it's not in the
database it can't go in an itinerary — so the DB must hold everything a visitor might
do/see/eat, not just activities.

## The tier system (governs content investment)

- **T1 Destination Draws** (Zip World, Snowdon, coasteering-in-Pembrokeshire): rich
  landing pages, SEO priority. Rule: if someone Googles it + "Wales", we should be page 1.
- **T2 Trip Enhancers** (castles, waterfalls, pubs, beaches): appear inside itineraries and
  region pages; standalone page only if real search demand.
- **T3 Practical Services** (gear shops, bike washes, parking): data points only.
  **T3 never gets its own URL.**

## Content quality bar

Every page: would a real person bookmark it? does it beat the first Google result? does it
help someone plan or book? Honesty converts ("this hike is hard and the weather will
probably be terrible"). Specific local knowledge is the moat.

## Competitive stance

Curated + verified vs Visit Wales (generic) and TripAdvisor (user-generated). Trip planning
is the core product neither has. See [[competitors]].

## Success metric

**Trips planned and bookings made**, not page views.
