## Why this operator matters

The Pen-y-Gwryd Hotel — "the PYG" to climbers — is **the hotel where the 1953 British Mount Everest expedition trained**, sleeping in the bunkrooms at the head of the Nant Gwynant valley while honing their rope work on the surrounding crags. Edmund Hillary, Tenzing Norgay (on a later visit), John Hunt and most of the rest of the team all signed the dining-room ceiling, which has been preserved unchanged for seventy years.

For Adventure Wales, the directory listing matters because:

1. **It's the named heritage anchor for the [Snowdonia climbing combo](/snowdonia).** The Joe Brown / Don Whillans British-climbing-birthplace narrative that lives in that combo's intro runs through the PYG — it's the physical building where that story happened.
2. **It's the pub-stop on every Snowdon Horseshoe day.** The Pyg Track to Snowdon takes its name from the hotel, and the hotel sits 200m from the Pen-y-Pass car park trailhead — every Snowdon-hiking itinerary on the site references it implicitly.

## What the hotel actually is (today)

- **Family-run since 1947** by the Pullee family (currently in the third generation under Rupert Pullee).
- **A working hotel and pub** — about 16 bedrooms, plus a separate climbers' bunkhouse, plus a public bar and dining room open to non-residents.
- **Open as a pub from approximately Easter through October** (winter opening is patchy — verify current season before recommending a drop-in).
- **Three-course evening meal** served at a fixed sitting time (typically 7:30pm gong) — the format hasn't changed in decades and is itself part of the experience.
- **The "Smoke Room"** — a snug bar at the back of the hotel, lined with mountaineering memorabilia (ice axes from the 1953 expedition, signed photographs, route diagrams) and the famous dining-room ceiling with the 1953 team signatures.

It is **not a luxury hotel** — bedrooms are pleasantly old-fashioned rather than five-star, and the appeal is the heritage and the location, not the spa. Set expectations accordingly.

## Durable framing facts (safe to use without re-verification)

- **The training base for the 1953 British Mount Everest expedition** — verified historically; the dining-room ceiling signatures are still on display.
- **John Hunt, Edmund Hillary, Tenzing Norgay (on later visits) and the wider 1953 team all stayed here.** Hunt's account in "The Ascent of Everest" (1953) describes the centre.
- **The Pyg Track to Yr Wyddfa (Snowdon) is named after the hotel** — Pyg = Pen-y-Gwryd (the path was used by hotel staff to reach the summit).
- **Founded as a coaching inn** for the Snowdon-Beddgelert road traffic in the 18th century; converted to a climbers' inn in the late Victorian period as Snowdonia opened up as a recreational mountain area.
- **The hotel's bar is the oldest continuously-operating mountain pub in Wales** by reasonable count — used by Welsh and English climbing parties since at least the 1880s.

## Pairing notes

- **The natural post-Snowdon stop.** Every Pyg Track / Miners' Track / Snowdon Horseshoe itinerary passes through Pen-y-Pass — the PYG is 200m down the road and is the standard pub stop on the way back.
- **The natural pre-climb dinner stop for Clogwyn / Lliwedd routes.** Climbers using the Pen-y-Pass YHA or the PYG bunkhouse as a base for the Clogwyn Du'r Arddu (Cloggy) routes typically eat at the PYG the night before.
- **Pair with the Snowdon Mountain Railway** for visitors who want the heritage angle without the climb — railway to the summit in the morning, PYG for dinner.
- **The hotel does not run guided activities itself** — it's accommodation and food, not an adventure operator. Don't list it under activity categories; do cross-reference it from the climbing combo, the Snowdon hiking pages, and any 1953-Everest / classic-Welsh-climbing editorial.
