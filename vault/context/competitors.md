# Competitive Landscape

> Context note for AI agents working on Adventure Wales (adventurewales.co.uk, pre-launch): trip-planning platform + verified operator directory for Welsh outdoor adventures. Revenue: operator subscriptions (£9.99/£29.99), affiliates, enquiry forwarding. SERP observations below are from live searches on 2026-07-06 (US-indexed results; UK SERPs will skew slightly more toward TripAdvisor/local packs). See [[market]] for sector numbers.

## Competitor-by-competitor

### Visit Wales (visitwales.com) — the incumbent authority
- **What**: Welsh Government DMO. Deep editorial on adventure ([adventure & activities hub](https://www.visitwales.com/things-do/adventure-and-activities)), regional guides, operator accreditation lists. No booking, no live availability, no operator self-service.
- **Strengths**: Government-grade domain authority; ranks page 1 for virtually every "activity + wales" head term (seen for coasteering, MTB, Snowdonia). Trusted brand.
- **Weaknesses**: Pure inspiration content; dead-ends at "find an operator yourself". No itineraries with bookable components, no pricing, no reviews. Slow-moving.
- **SEO position**: Dominant on head terms; weak on transactional long-tail ("coasteering stag do tenby") and comparison queries. Treat as a link/partnership target, not a beatable rival on head terms.

### TripAdvisor
- **What**: Reviews + "things to do" listings; Viator-powered booking where inventory exists.
- **Strengths**: Ranked **#4 for "things to do in snowdonia"** in our live search ([their page](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attractions-g186445-Activities-Snowdonia_Eryri_National_Park_North_Wales_Wales.html)); huge review corpus; owns "best X in Y" queries.
- **Weaknesses**: **Absent from page 1 of "coasteering pembrokeshire" and "mountain biking wales"** in our searches. Small rural Welsh activity operators are thinly/inaccurately listed; attraction-centric (castles, railways) not trip-centric; stale content.
- **SEO position**: Strong on destination "things to do", weak on activity-specific Welsh terms — that's the gap.

### GetYourGuide / Viator — OTAs, thin Wales inventory
- **What**: Global experience OTAs, 15–30% commission. Wales coverage checked live: mostly **coach day-trips INTO Wales from Manchester/London/Chester/Liverpool** ([GYG example](https://www.getyourguide.com/manchester-l1128/from-manchester-north-wales-snowdonia-and-chester-tour-t307008/)), plus a modest set of Snowdonia adventures (rafting, sunrise Snowdon hikes, Llyn Padarn kayaking, canyoning) on [Viator](https://www.viator.com/Snowdonia/d24144-ttd).
- **Strengths**: Conversion machine, review volume, growing share (OTAs = 37% of experience bookings 2025 — [Arival](https://arival.travel/article/otas-capture-one-third-experiences-bookings/)).
- **Weaknesses**: Coverage of genuine Welsh adventure operators is sparse — most coasteering/gorge/caving providers aren't on them (commission-averse, small capacity). No trip planning, no local depth. Didn't appear on page 1 of any of our three money-term searches.
- **SEO**: Strong globally, weak on Welsh adventure long-tail. Their commission model is Adventure Wales's main pricing foil.

### Much Better Adventures
- **What**: UK marketplace for hosted multi-day adventure holidays (~160 trips, 49 countries), **8% commission** model; raised £5m from Gresham House Dec 2024 ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/much-better-adventures-funding), [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/12/06/much-better-adventures-raises-5m-for-self-led-travel/)); revenue ~$1.7M (2024) growing fast ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/much-better-adventures), figures vary by source).
- **Strengths**: Brand affinity with exactly Adventure Wales's audience; proven that low-commission adventure marketplaces work; strong content/SEO for "adventure holidays" queries.
- **Weaknesses vs AW**: Multi-day hosted international trips only — barely any Wales inventory, no day activities, no directory. Complementary more than competitive; a model to copy (curated supply, editorial voice) not fight.

### AllTrails / Komoot (trail apps)
- **What**: Route discovery/navigation. AllTrails 60m+ users; Komoot ~40m users, ranked page 1 for "mountain biking wales" ([komoot Wales MTB guide](https://www.komoot.com/guide/67400/mtb-trails-in-wales)); Trailforks also page 1.
- **Strengths**: Own self-guided hiking/MTB route queries; massive UGC; app lock-in.
- **Weaknesses**: Zero operator/guided-activity inventory, zero trip planning beyond routes. **Komoot was gutted post-acquisition — bought by Bending Spoons ~€300m (Mar 2025), ~85% of ~150 staff laid off, price rises, community trust damaged** ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/komoot-acquisition), [Escape Collective](https://escapecollective.com/how-komoot-lost-its-way/)) — a wobble worth exploiting on UK trail content.
- **Positioning**: Don't compete on GPS/routes; link out. Compete on "what guided things can I *book* around this trail".

### Day Out With The Kids (and similar listing sites: Days Out Info, Kids Days Out)
- **What**: UK's biggest family days-out directory — claims **35m+ annual visits, 8,000+ listings**; monetises via **paid listing tiers + marketing packages + ticketing commission** ([their sales page](https://www.dayoutwiththekids.co.uk/work-with-us)).
- **Strengths**: Proof the paid-listing model works at scale in UK leisure; huge family SEO footprint.
- **Weaknesses vs AW**: Attraction/family-centric, not adventure; thin per-listing quality; no trip planning; Wales adventure operators poorly covered. Their pricing validates AW's subscription tiers.

### Beyonk (and Bokun/FareHarbor etc.) — booking SaaS, not a marketplace
- **What**: UK visitor-experience **booking software** (ticketing, EPOS, reseller network) used by many small UK operators ([beyonk.com](https://beyonk.com/)); merged with BookingHound.
- **Relationship to AW**: Not a demand channel — no meaningful consumer SEO. Potential **integration partner**: many target operators already run Beyonk/Bokun checkout, so AW enquiry-forwarding/affiliate links can deep-link into their widgets. Also a competitor for operator wallet-share (SaaS fees).

### AdventureSmart Wales / mbwales.com / regional DMOs
- **AdventureSmart** ([adventuresmart.uk/wales](https://www.adventuresmart.uk/wales/)): safety-messaging campaign, not a directory — align with it, don't fight it.
- **MBWales.com**: ranked **#1 for "mountain biking wales"** — official niche hub; shows single-activity portals can beat Visit Wales. mtb.wales also page 1.
- **Visit Pembrokeshire / Visit Snowdonia / Visit Mid Wales / Eryri NP / Pembrokeshire Coast NP / NRW**: all take page-1 slots on regional activity terms. Directory-style but static, no booking, no operator tooling.

## Live SERP snapshots (2026-07-06)

- **"coasteering pembrokeshire"**: page 1 = operators (Celtic Quest #1, Outer Reef, TYF, Preseli Venture) + DMOs (Visit Pembrokeshire, Visit Wales, PCNP, Pembrokeshire Outdoor Charter) + Yumping (weak aggregator) + FBM Holidays blog. **No TripAdvisor, no GYG/Viator, no strong comparison/directory page** → an honest "compare all Pembrokeshire coasteering providers" page has a real shot.
- **"things to do in snowdonia"**: page 1 = personal travel blogs (Helen in Wonderlust #1, Oh What A Knight), Visit Wales, TripAdvisor, Visit Snowdonia, Eryri NP, National Trust. Blogs ranking #1 shows depth+freshness beats DA here — but this term is crowded; target "snowdonia itinerary"/"adventure weekend snowdonia" variants instead.
- **"mountain biking wales"**: page 1 = MBWales.com, Visit Wales, NRW, Komoot, mtb.wales, Antur Stiniog, Visit Mid Wales, Trailforks, BikeRadar. Hardest of the three; enter via trail-centre + guided-MTB long-tail, not the head term.

## Where Adventure Wales can win

1. **Trip-level planning is unowned.** Nobody assembles "3-day Pembrokeshire adventure: coasteering + sea kayak + where to stay/eat" with bookable operators. Visit Wales inspires, blogs describe, OTAs sell fragments, trail apps navigate — no one plans the trip. This is the wedge and the itinerary content moat.
2. **Operator dashboards + flat pricing.** Small Welsh operators face 15–30% OTA commission or invisibility; DOWTK proves they'll pay for listings. £9.99/£29.99 + enquiry forwarding undercuts everyone; add value (analytics, availability, review capture) to retain.
3. **Honest local depth.** Verified, current operator data (the launch gate's 18 verified operators) beats TripAdvisor's stale listings and Yumping-style scraped aggregators; "birthplace of coasteering" storytelling beats OTA boilerplate.
4. **Long-tail SEO gaps**: activity×region combos ("gorge walking brecon beacons", "sea kayaking anglesey"), comparison pages ("best coasteering providers pembrokeshire"), and trip-shaped queries — where page 1 is currently blogs, thin aggregators, or nothing.
5. **Threats to respect**: Visit Wales's domain authority on head terms (partner, don't fight); OTA share growth (37% and rising) if GYG/Viator ever go deep on Wales; single-activity hubs (MBWales) out-focusing AW in one vertical; Google AI-overview compression of informational traffic — argues for tools/directory utility over pure articles.
