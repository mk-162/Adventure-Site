# Welsh & UK Adventure Tourism Market

> Context note for AI agents working on Adventure Wales (adventurewales.co.uk) — a pre-launch trip-planning platform + operator directory for outdoor adventures in Wales. Researched 2026-07-06 via web search. Flag: several market-size figures come from commercial research houses and disagree wildly; treat directionally.

## 1. Market size & growth

**UK adventure tourism** — estimates diverge by ~3x depending on definition:
- [Future Market Insights](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/united-kingdom-adventure-tourism-market): USD **13.7bn (2025) → 23.8bn (2035)**, 5.6% CAGR. Soft adventure (hiking, cycling, wildlife) = **72%** of 2025 market; hard adventure (climbing, watersports) = 28%.
- [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/adventure-tourism-market/uk): USD 38.4bn (2024) → 93.8bn (2030), 16.4% CAGR — much broader definition. Uncertain; use FMI as the conservative anchor.
- UK is the **largest adventure tourism market in Europe**, with ~19% of the world's adventure travellers; a VisitBritain survey found **40% of British tourists prefer sports/active holidays** ([trade.gov overview](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/united-kingdom-adventure-tourism-market-overview)).

**Wales visitor economy** ([Wales Visitor Economy Profile 2024, gov.wales](https://www.gov.wales/wales-visitor-economy-profile-2024-html)):
- Tourism industries = **£3.8bn GVA (5.1% of Wales GVA, 2022 data)**; **159,000 jobs (11.8% of Welsh employment)**; 12,625 tourism enterprises.
- Domestic (GB) overnight trips 2024: **7.61m trips, 22.3m nights, £2.24bn spend** ([gov.wales](https://www.gov.wales/domestic-gb-tourism-statistics-overnight-trips-wales-2024-html)). Avg trip: 2.9 nights, £295. Vs 2023: volume **−10%, spend +11%** — fewer trips, higher value.
- Day trips: 11.47m trips / £467m in Q1 2025 alone ([gov.wales](https://www.gov.wales/domestic-gb-tourism-statistics-january-march-2025-html)).
- Inbound: **445k international visits / £203m spend in H1 2024** (+10% visits vs 2023); ~1m visits/yr forecast. Wales gets the **lowest inbound volumes of any UK nation/region except NE England** ([gov.wales](https://www.gov.wales/international-inbound-visits-and-spend-wales-html)) → the market is overwhelmingly **domestic GB** (~85–90% of spend).

**The Adventure Wales-specific number** — Welsh outdoor activity sector evaluation (Miller Research for Pembrokeshire Coastal Forum + **Wales Adventure Tourism Organisation**, spring 2023, 600+ respondents):
- Commercially-led outdoor activity: **£272.9m net annual economic impact**; including self-led participation: **£1.6bn/yr, ~31,000 jobs (~21% of all Welsh tourism jobs)** ([WSA summary](https://wsa.wales/research-indicates-huge-value-of-outdoor-activity-to-wales/), [PCF report](https://www.pembrokeshirecoastalforum.org.uk/economic-and-social-evaluation-of-the-outdoor-activity-sector-in-wales/)).
- 94% visitor satisfaction with Welsh outdoor experiences; primary participation motive has shifted toward **mental health / wellbeing**.
- Data is 2023-vintage — the best available sector-level figure as of mid-2026.

## 2. Policy & Visit Wales strategy

- [Welcome to Wales: Priorities for the Visitor Economy 2020–2025](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2020-02/welcome-to-wales-priorities-for-the-visitor-economy-2020-2025.pdf): ambition for Wales to be known for "**world-leading adventure**, creative culture and language, and outstanding protected landscapes". Core challenges named: **seasonality, spend and spread**. Funds: £10m Brilliant Basics (infrastructure), £50m Wales Tourism Investment Fund. As of July 2026 no fully-published successor strategy was found in searches — verify before citing post-2025 policy.
- **Visitor levy**: [Visitor Accommodation (Register and Levy) Etc. (Wales) Act 2025](https://www.gov.wales/visitor-accommodation-register-and-levy-etc-wales-act-2025) became law Sept 2025. All Welsh visitor accommodation must register with the Welsh Revenue Authority from **autumn 2026**; councils may charge **75p–£1.30 pp/night from 2027 at earliest**. Mild cost headwind for operators/accommodation; the register may become a useful public dataset.
- Welsh Government has made "continued investment in the Wales adventure sector" a named priority ([gov.wales](https://www.gov.wales/continued-investment-wales-adventure-sector)) — Zip World-style "reputation-changing products" are the flagship model.

## 3. Demand patterns

- **Heavily seasonal**: Easter–September peak; Visit Wales' own strategy targets seasonality as a structural weakness, and [Tourism Barometer waves](https://www.gov.wales/tourism-barometer-february-wave-2025-html) show spring 2024/25 bookings perceived as "slow", with North Wales strongest in self-catering.
- **Domestic-first**: GB residents dominate; inbound is small and concentrated (Wales captures little of London-gateway inbound). Post-2024 trend: **fewer but higher-spending trips** (2024: −10% volume, +11% spend).
- Post-pandemic tailwinds: wellbeing-motivated participation, soft adventure growth (72% of market), and 74% of Britons planning travel in 2025 with ~20% preferring adventure-focused trips ([Adventure Brits](https://adventurebrits.co.uk/adventure-tourism-boom/), secondary source).

## 5. Industry bodies (partnership/credibility targets)

- **Wales Adventure Tourism Organisation (WATO)** — the Welsh adventure trade body; co-led the £1.6bn sector evaluation; runs [accreditation recognised by Visit Wales](https://www.visitwales.com/WATO/accreditation-schemes).
- **Visit Wales AAAS** (Adventure Activity Assurance schemes) + **Adventuremark**; **AHOEC** ([ahoec.org](https://ahoec.org/), 170+ UK outdoor centres, Gold Standard scheme recognised by Visit Wales).
- **AdventureSmart Wales/UK** ([adventuresmart.uk](https://www.adventuresmart.uk/wales/)) — multi-agency *safety messaging* campaign (born in Wales 2017, Welsh Gov funded), NOT a directory/competitor; partners incl. Snowdonia-Active, **SWOAPG** (South Wales Outdoor Activity Providers Group), The Outdoor Partnership.
- **ATTA** ([adventuretravel.biz](https://www.adventuretravel.biz/)) — global trade association; relevant for research/standards, less for Wales ops. Also: Wales Tourism Alliance, North Wales Tourism, regional DMOs.

## 6. How adventure travellers plan & book ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/otas-capture-one-third-experiences-bookings/) data, global operators)

- **OTA share of experience bookings: 37% in 2025** (28% in 2023, 33% in 2024) — OTAs rising fast; operator **direct-website share fell 29% → 25%** 2024→25 ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/direct-bookings-dive-otas-rise/), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/otas-increased-booking-share-experiences-arival-report)).
- Online penetration for experiences: 17% (2019) → **33% (2025)** → projected 42% by 2029. Total online (incl. direct) ~60% of bookings.
- OTA commissions run **15–30%** ([Bokun](https://www.bokun.io/ota-bookings)) — the wedge that makes a £9.99/£29.99 flat subscription attractive to small Welsh operators.
- Caveat: most small Welsh coasteering/kayak operators still take the bulk of bookings direct (own site/phone), discovered via Google — SEO position is the real battleground (see [[competitors]]).

## Key takeaways for Adventure Wales

1. Real addressable anchor: **£272.9m commercially-led Welsh outdoor activity sector** (not the headline £1.6bn, which includes self-led).
2. Market is **domestic, seasonal, and higher-spend-per-trip** — product should optimise for GB weekenders planning Apr–Sep trips.
3. Activity ownership is strongly regional (coasteering=Pembrokeshire, mountain/zip=Eryri, gorge/caving=Brecon) — matches a region×activity content architecture.
4. OTAs are taking share (37%) at 15–30% commission → clear pitch for subscription + direct-enquiry model.
5. WATO, AAAS/Adventuremark accreditation and AdventureSmart alignment are cheap credibility wins.
